# NestWise — Product Requirements Document (PRD)

**Product Name:** NestWise  
**Document Type:** Product Requirements Document  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Primary Platforms:** Responsive Web Application (desktop, tablet, mobile), designed to support future native mobile apps  
**Core Audience:** Expecting mothers, expecting partners/fathers, new parents, children, and families  
**Product Theme:** Pregnancy → Birth → Parenting → Child Development & Learning

---

## 1. Product Vision

NestWise is a family companion that supports parents from pregnancy through childbirth and throughout a child's development up to age 12.

The product should combine:

- Stage-based education
- Personalized pregnancy guidance
- Partner/father guidance
- Nutrition and exercise guidance
- AI-assisted questions and explanations
- Birth preparation
- Postpartum education
- Newborn-care tutorials
- Parent education
- Child-development tracking
- Age-appropriate children's learning activities
- Quizzes and games
- Family activities
- Helpful reminders, facts, books, articles, and resources

NestWise should feel supportive, calm, educational, easy to understand, and family-friendly rather than clinical or overwhelming.

> **Important product principle:** NestWise provides educational information and decision support, not medical diagnosis or emergency care. Medical features must clearly distinguish general educational guidance from situations requiring a qualified healthcare professional or emergency services.

---

# 2. Product Goals

## 2.1 Primary Goals

1. Guide expecting parents through every stage of pregnancy.
2. Give mothers and partners role-specific, actionable information.
3. Help parents prepare confidently for childbirth.
4. Prepare parents for postpartum recovery and newborn care.
5. Continue supporting families after birth instead of ending at delivery.
6. Teach parents about children's physical, cognitive, emotional, social, and behavioral development.
7. Provide age-appropriate educational activities for children.
8. Encourage healthy parent-child bonding and family participation.
9. Personalize content using pregnancy stage, child's age, preferences, dietary restrictions, and other relevant profile information.
10. Make reliable information easy to discover without overwhelming users.

## 2.2 Secondary Goals

- Encourage consistent learning through daily facts and short activities.
- Track progress through lessons, quizzes, games, and milestones.
- Give partners concrete ways to participate.
- Make educational content visually engaging.
- Build a platform that can grow from pregnancy support into a long-term family product.

---

# 3. Core Product Structure

The application should have three major journeys.

### Part 1 — Pregnancy Journey
From discovering/entering pregnancy through the final weeks before delivery.

### Part 2 — Birth & Postpartum
Preparation for labor, hospital, childbirth, immediate postpartum recovery, and the first days/weeks after birth.

### Part 3 — Baby, Child & Parent Learning
Newborn care followed by development and learning from birth through age 12.

The application should automatically transition the family's home dashboard as their stage changes.

# 5. Onboarding

The onboarding experience should be simple and warm.

## 5.1 Initial Questions

Ask the user:

1. What brings you to NestWise?
   - We are expecting a baby
   - Our baby has arrived
   - I want parenting/child-development guidance

2. What is your role?
   - Mother
   - Partner / Father
   - Parent / Caregiver

3. Pregnancy stage, if applicable:
   - Due date
   - Current pregnancy week
   - Current pregnancy month

The system should automatically convert between weeks and approximate months.

Example:

**8 weeks — approximately 2 months**

Pregnancy content should primarily use gestational weeks because months do not map perfectly to pregnancy weeks.

4. If baby has arrived:
   - Child date of birth
   - Optional name/nickname

5. Optional personalization:
   - Dietary preference
   - Food allergies/intolerances
   - Preferred learning format
   - Content interests
   - Reminder preferences

Do not require unnecessary sensitive information.

# 11. Ask NestWise — AI Assistant

NestWise should contain an AI-assisted Q&A experience.

Example questions:

- Why am I feeling tired this week?
- Is nausea common at this stage?
- What foods contain iron?
- What should my partner learn before delivery?
- What should I pack for the hospital?
- How do I burp a newborn?

## 11.1 Context-Aware Answers

When appropriate, the assistant can use:

- Pregnancy week
- Parent role
- Child age
- Dietary preferences
- Allergies explicitly provided for personalization
- Current NestWise module

## 11.2 Safety Requirements

The AI must:

- Never claim to diagnose a medical condition.
- Avoid presenting uncertain information as fact.
- Clearly identify emergency warning signs where relevant.
- Recommend contacting an appropriate healthcare professional when a question exceeds general educational guidance.
- Avoid medication/dosage recommendations unless the feature is specifically built around verified clinical sources and appropriate safety review.
- Prefer evidence-based sources.
- Display sources/references for health-related educational answers when possible.

Create an obvious emergency disclaimer:

**NestWise is not an emergency service. If you believe you or your baby may be in immediate danger, contact local emergency services or your healthcare provider.**

# 50. AI Architecture Requirements

AI should not be allowed to answer health questions without guardrails.

Recommended flow:

```text
User Question
      ↓
Determine category
      ↓
Pregnancy / Birth / Postpartum / Baby / Parenting / General
      ↓
Retrieve relevant reviewed NestWise content
      ↓
Generate answer grounded in retrieved sources
      ↓
Medical-safety classification
      ↓
Add appropriate caution/escalation
      ↓
Display answer + references
```

Prefer a retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) architecture using curated content rather than relying exclusively on unrestricted model knowledge.

AI should know contextual information only when the user has chosen to provide it and it is necessary for personalization.

# 51. Safety Architecture

Health-related questions can be classified into broad levels.

## Level 1 — General Education

Example:

> Why is fatigue common in early pregnancy?

Provide educational information.

## Level 2 — Symptom / Personalized Health Concern

Example:

> I have been vomiting all day.

Provide cautious general information, highlight relevant warning signs, and encourage appropriate professional guidance instead of diagnosing.

## Level 3 — Potential Emergency

Examples involving severe bleeding, breathing difficulty, loss of consciousness, seizures, severe chest pain, or other potentially urgent symptoms should trigger a prominent recommendation to seek immediate professional/emergency assistance.

Exact escalation logic and wording should be developed with qualified medical review and localized for the user's country.

# 52. Privacy & Child Safety

NestWise may process pregnancy and children's information, making privacy a core requirement.

Requirements:

- Collect only necessary data.
- Clear consent and privacy explanations.
- Secure authentication.
- Encrypt sensitive data in transit and at rest.
- Allow account/data deletion.
- Allow parents to remove child profiles and uploaded media.
- Do not sell sensitive health or children's data.
- Minimize third-party tracking.
- Separate child-facing and parent-facing experiences.
- Avoid public child profiles by default.
- Build age-appropriate experiences and parental controls.
- Review applicable privacy and children's-data laws before launch.

If deployed in regulated healthcare contexts, obtain legal/compliance review rather than assuming that a particular healthcare regulation automatically applies.

# 64. Final Product Principle

NestWise should feel like a family learning companion that grows alongside the family:

**Pregnancy → Birth → Newborn → Toddler → Child → Family Learning**

At every stage, the interface should answer three simple questions:

1. **What is happening right now?**
2. **What should I know?**
3. **What can we do together?**

The goal is not to replace doctors, teachers, or parents. The goal is to help families feel more prepared, informed, connected, and confident throughout the journey.
